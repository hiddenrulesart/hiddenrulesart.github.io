The story

In the 1950s the English scientist Lewis Fry Richardson wanted to know whether the length of the border between two countries affected how likely they were to go to war. He found that published figures disagreed wildly: Spain put its border with Portugal at 987 km, Portugal said 1,214 km. Nobody had made a mistake. Measuring with a shorter ruler follows more bays and bends, so the answer keeps growing. Plot length against ruler size on log scales and you get a straight line.

In 1967 Benoît Mandelbrot asked "How long is the coast of Britain?" and explained the slope of that line as a fractional dimension: about 1.25 for Britain's west coast, close to 1 for smooth South Africa, more for fjord-cut Norway. A curve with dimension above 1 is more than a line but less than an area. The Koch snowflake (1904) is the perfect example: dimension log 4 / log 3 ≈ 1.2619, and infinite length around a finite area.

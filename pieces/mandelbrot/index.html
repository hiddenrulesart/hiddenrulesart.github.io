The story
z → z² + c

Pick a number c. Start at z = 0, square it, add c, and repeat. Either the numbers stay small forever, or they run off to infinity. The Mandelbrot set is every c that stays small.

Robert Brooks and Peter Matelski printed a rough picture of it in 1978. In 1980 Benoit Mandelbrot, working at IBM, had computers draw it in detail, and the strange specks around it turned out to be tiny copies of the whole thing. In 1982 Adrien Douady and John Hubbard proved all of it is connected: every speck is joined to the rest by thin filaments.

The top-left inset shows the Julia set for the c under your cursor: inside the Mandelbrot set it is one connected piece; outside, it shatters into dust. And the line through the middle is the logistic map from episode 3: the bulbs along it are the period doublings, and the Feigenbaum point preset is where they pile up.

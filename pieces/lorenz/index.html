The story
dx/dt = σ(y − x)
dy/dt = x(ρ − z) − y
dz/dt = xy − βz

In 1961 the meteorologist Edward Lorenz re-ran a weather simulation, typing in a number rounded from 0.506127 to 0.506. The forecast came out completely different. Two years later he boiled a model of rising warm air down to these three equations and found that the motion never repeats, yet always stays on this shape: a strange attractor. In 1972 he asked: does the flap of a butterfly's wings in Brazil set off a tornado in Texas? The attractor even looks like a butterfly. Try changing ρ: below about 24.7 the chaos dies and every particle settles down.

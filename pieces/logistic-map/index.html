The story
xnext = r · x · (1 − x)

A toy model of an animal population: x is the population (as a fraction of the maximum), r is how fast it breeds. In 1976 the ecologist Robert May showed that this one line can behave wildly: for small r the population settles down; past 3 it bounces between 2 values, then 4, 8, 16… and past about 3.57 it never repeats: chaos.

In 1975 Mitchell Feigenbaum, playing with a pocket calculator, noticed that each doubling arrives 4.669… times sooner than the last. Then he found the same number in completely different equations, and experiments later found it in real fluids. Zoom in anywhere chaotic and you'll find tiny copies of the whole picture.
